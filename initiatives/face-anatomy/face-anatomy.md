# face-anatomy — mocap-studio

**Status:** Proposed by Rafael, 2026-11-02. This is a **research handoff**: no
epics, no tasks, nothing built. The next session researches and brings back
options. It doesn't start implementing.

**Goal:** make the character move like a person, not only look like one, through
a rig and armature based on anatomy, with some physics for the muscles.

**Why (Rafael):** "o photorealismo está somente na imagem, mas não em todos os
trejeitos humanos". He expects to stop adding detail at some point, but not yet:
"até o momento acho que podemos aumentar os detalhes do nosso personagem".

## What Rafael asked the next session to do

1. **Research three repositories:** Z-Anatomy, MuSkeMo and anatomy3d-blender.
2. **Improve the rigging and armature:** models based on anatomy.
3. **Add physics for the muscles:** his starting idea is to model them as liquids
   with variable viscosity, "ou alguma outra coisa nesse sentido".
4. **Solve the inverse problem:** he frames it as inverse kinematics, a classic
   physics problem: how much one muscle contracts and another extends to make a
   smile.

What the character already has, checked on 2026-11-02:

- **Armature:** a metarig of 185 bones that deforms the mesh, including `jaw`,
  `chin`, `lip.T.*`, `lip.B.*`, `cheek.*`, `nose*` and `tongue*`, with matching
  vertex groups. The generated Rigify control rig isn't connected to the mesh.
- **Shapes:** the 52 ARKit shapes, generated by MPFB rather than made for this
  face, plus MPFB's character targets.
- **Bones already driving a pose:** the F pose pulls the `lip.B.*` bones back and
  up, and L and T raise the tongue bones. These values are a first pass.
- **Not used yet:** the other lip, chin and cheek bones.

## The three repositories (first reading only, 2026-11-02)

I read their READMEs, license data and file names, not their code. Check these
before relying on them.

| Repository | What it appears to be | License | First reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Z-Anatomy](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy) | Blender template used as an anatomy atlas, derived from BodyParts3D. The README doesn't say whether it's rigged | CC BY-SA 4.0, BodyParts3D CC BY-SA 2.1 JP; **some parts are NC** (inner ear, kidney) | Possibly useful as visual reference for where muscles attach |
| [MuSkeMo](https://github.com/PashavanBijlert/MuSkeMo) | Biomechanics models for OpenSim: rigid bodies, joints, muscles as paths with moment arms. Tested on Blender up to 5.2. The README doesn't mention faces or mesh deformation | **No license found** | Limbs and bones, not skin; a path-muscle may not suit lips, which have no bone to pull against |
| [anatomy3d-blender](https://github.com/Freedom-of-Form-Foundation/anatomy3d-blender) | Unfinished plugin for Blender 3.3, last commit April 2023. File names show only `assets/muscle_geometry_nodes.blend` for muscles, nothing for the face | GPL-2.0 | Possibly worth reading for its geometry-nodes approach |

**Ask Rafael before downloading or using these, under the license rule:**

- **Z-Anatomy:** the rule bans NC material even in private tests, and the NC parts
  may be inside `Z-Anatomy.zip`. CC BY-SA isn't on the rule's allowed list either,
  and meshes derived from it would inherit share-alike. Looking at it for reference
  is a different case from deriving from it, and that needs his decision.
- **MuSkeMo:** no license means no permission by default. Asking the author is one
  option.

## Problems to bring back, not settle alone

These are my concerns, for Rafael to weigh. None of them is a decision.

1. **Where the muscle signal comes from.** A muscle model needs to know how much
   each muscle fires. OpenSeeFace gives 66 points and no tongue. Solving muscle
   activations from them is the inverse problem Rafael describes, and it has more
   muscles than measurements, so many solutions fit. The session has to say what
   picks one.
2. **Inverse kinematics or something else.** IK usually solves joint angles from
   end positions. Solving forces or activations is closer to inverse dynamics or
   optimization. Facial muscle activation from sparse markers has been published
   (from memory, unverified: Sifakis, Neverov and Fedkiw, SIGGRAPH 2005). Check
   this and whether any code has a usable license.
3. **Liquid or something else.** From memory, unverified: muscle is usually
   modeled as a nearly incompressible soft solid, or with Hill-type force models.
   Variable-viscosity fluid is Rafael's idea and deserves a fair comparison. Also
   say what Blender can really do here, such as soft body, cloth, geometry nodes
   simulation, or an external solver.
4. **Cost against the current result.** Is a full muscle model needed, or would a
   simpler step get most of the gain? Candidates are lip and cheek bones,
   corrective shapes, jiggle and delay on soft tissue, and skin wrinkles driven by
   the face. The fader test showed that better timing and shapes carried much of
   the gain without any anatomy.
5. **When to stop.** Rafael expects to stop at some point, and has only a few hours
   a week. Ask him what would count as enough before scoping.
